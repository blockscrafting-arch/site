## [The harsh reality of UX design job market](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ux-case-study-minto-pyramid-structure-guide\#a17c1c18011b4886b4866279b38d5d7a "The harsh reality of UX design job market") **The harsh reality of UX design job market**

According to the [State of Design Job Market 2019](https://adplist.substack.com/p/state-of-the-design-job-market), only about 67.9% of designers landed a new job within three months. Fast forward to 2024, the number decreased to 49.5%.

_The market is saturated, and competition is fierce._

![Illustration by Live Data Technologies.](https://www.notion.so/image/attachment%3A58558e96-f91a-439d-b6c0-a61897a3f4e5%3Aimage.png?table=block&id=558c4f75-abd1-4657-81cf-4c512e61eaba&cache=v2)Illustration by Live Data Technologies.

We're not just competing locally anymore—we're up against global talent, [AI tools](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ai-tools-ux-design), and [an industry that's evolving faster than ever](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ai-ux-design-jobs). Creating a standout [UX portfolio](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/how-to-format-ux-portfolios) and compelling [UX case study](https://uxplaybook.org/faq/how-to-write-ux-case-study) has never been more crucial.

And yet, most of us are still playing by the old rules.

## [UX case study: The silent portfolio dealbreaker](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ux-case-study-minto-pyramid-structure-guide\#03c69481c53341ea8b4d27c13757e3ae "UX case study: The silent portfolio dealbreaker") UX case study: The silent portfolio dealbreaker

Your UX design case study is not just another UX portfolio piece, it’s your [ticket to an interview](https://uxplaybook.org/faq/how-do-i-pass-first-round-of-ux-job-interviews). [Hiring managers](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/hiring-manager-advice-ux-design-interviews) skim dozens, if not hundreds of case studies.

**If yours doesn’t stand out, it gets ignored.**

Most designers think they’re [showcasing their best work](https://uxplaybook.org/faq/choose-the-right-projects-for-your-portfolio), but in reality, many case studies fail to answer key hiring questions:

1. _What problem did you solve?_

2. _How did your design impact users?_

3. _Why does it matter to the business?_

4. _What decisions made the biggest difference?_

![How UX designers write their portfolios meme.](https://www.notion.so/image/attachment%3A86aceb24-58ca-4dc0-a603-e9661417d118%3Aimage.png?table=block&id=6fea8916-55f2-4f35-9f40-e00fb6d48f74&cache=v2)How UX designers write their portfolios meme.

Without clear, compelling answers, your case study is just another pretty layout with no substance. A weak case study doesn’t just make you look unremarkable, it actively hurts your chances of landing the job.

## [Common mistakes of UX case study](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ux-case-study-minto-pyramid-structure-guide\#870c4edb29ca4cfcacf8cb062fa26485 "Common mistakes of UX case study") Common mistakes of UX case study

I used to think following the "proven formula" was the way to go:

✓ Clean Medium article

✓ Sleek Behance portfolio

✓ Active [Dribbble](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/why-designers-are-leaving-dribbble) presence

✓ Standard case study template

But here's what I learned the hard way: when you follow the same playbook as everyone else, you become _invisible_.

![5 mistakes that cost you job opportunities ](https://www.notion.so/image/attachment%3Abdbc06b2-5e32-4447-a76a-647724fd3098%3Aimage.png?table=block&id=682b311b-d60b-4e94-8e91-9fd7742b71bb&cache=v2)5 mistakes that cost you job opportunities

When crafting your UX design portfolio and UX case studies, avoid these 5 mistakes:

### [1. UX case study length](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ux-case-study-minto-pyramid-structure-guide\#1bd0d7f7bf12470893b38b089cdd5102 "1. UX case study length") 1\. UX case study length

❌ Show 47 [wireframe](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/how-to-ux-wireframe) versions

❌ Write 3,000+ word case studies

❌ Include every single design iteration

❌ [Document](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ux-living-documents) every stakeholder [meeting](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/how-to-have-better-ux-design-meetings)

💡

**Truth**: Hiring managers spend an average of 60 seconds per [case study](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/how-to-create-ux-case-study). That's not a typo—it's one minute. Your 15-minute read time is actively working against you.

### [2. UX design process](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ux-case-study-minto-pyramid-structure-guide\#9d3ddd69eaaa47fca2f761996dae6184 "2. UX design process") 2\. UX design process

❌ Show pretty final designs

❌ List generic [UX processes](https://uxplaybook.org/faq/what-is-design-process)

❌ Skip over the hard decisions

❌ Use buzzwords without context

💡

**Truth**: Any junior designer can follow a process. What hiring managers want to see is your thinking and how you handle tradeoffs.

### [3. UX writing impact](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ux-case-study-minto-pyramid-structure-guide\#6d555582e9cc4650b4bc2e643bd8f49c "3. UX writing impact") 3\. UX writing impact

❌ Rush the writing

❌ Skip proofreading

❌ Write like they're texting

❌ Use inconsistent [terminology](https://uxplaybook.org/ux-glossary)

💡

**Truth:** Poor writing makes hiring managers question your attention to detail and [communication skills](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/how-to-improve-communication-skills-as-a-ux-designer) \- crucial for any UX role.

## [Why does storytelling matter in UX case studies? ](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ux-case-study-minto-pyramid-structure-guide\#60e304973241423aa98b1c96fd727a6e "Why does storytelling matter in UX case studies? ") Why does storytelling matter in UX case studies?

Hiring managers spend less than 60 seconds skimming your case study before deciding whether to keep reading. That means you have to hook them fast.

A compelling UX design case study does not only showcase interfaces, it's about crafting a narrative that communicates impact. [Your UX portfolio should tell a story](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/how-to-tell-a-story-in-ux-case-study) that resonates.

Here are 3 reasons why storytelling matters:

![Power of UX storytelling.](https://www.notion.so/image/attachment%3Af8fe42d2-db2e-402c-844f-07e974805ece%3AWhy_storytelling_matters.png?table=block&id=1df2a971-edb7-806d-b41a-d646d19a9cb1&cache=v2)Power of UX storytelling.

#### [1. It makes your work memorable ](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ux-case-study-minto-pyramid-structure-guide\#0700aff7bb3b4200acddb1e727f36fc9 "1. It makes your work memorable ") **1\. It makes your work memorable**

A hiring manager reviewing dozens of portfolios will remember a strong story over a collection of screenshots.

#### [2. It highlights your decision-making process ](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ux-case-study-minto-pyramid-structure-guide\#63c160a205a24dfd8fe5af39575c82c0 "2. It highlights your decision-making process ") **2\. It highlights your decision-making process**

UX is not just about aesthetics; it’s about problem-solving. A good story explains why you made key decisions, not just what you did.

#### [3. It connects design to business outcomes](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ux-case-study-minto-pyramid-structure-guide\#b133fdec24b94dbb95cdbed4001553b0 "3. It connects design to business outcomes") **3\. It connects design to business outcomes**

Companies don’t hire designers just to make things look nice. They hire to solve business problems. A compelling story ties your design decisions to measurable impact.

💡

**Remember**: When done right, storytelling turns your case study into a compelling pitch, one that not only showcases your UX skills but also proves your ability to drive real business impact.

## [The Minto Pyramid Principle](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ux-case-study-minto-pyramid-structure-guide\#42e6aa2f8a7046debef801c999d6c5df "The Minto Pyramid Principle") **The Minto Pyramid Principle**

This framework, developed by [Barbara Minto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Minto), is widely used in business and consulting to structure clear, persuasive communication. Applied to UX case studies, it ensures clarity, engagement, and impact.

![Minto Pyramid Principle *Source: Simon Whatley  ](https://www.notion.so/image/attachment%3Acd127147-05fe-425c-abf8-2cb6cfe1d047%3Aimage.png?table=block&id=58d63c11-1cd7-4b0a-ab90-1a600a652e1f&cache=v2)Minto Pyramid Principle \* _Source:_ _[Simon Whatley](https://www.simonwhatley.co.uk/writing/applying-the-minto-pyramid-principle-to-ux-design/)_

💡

**NOTE**: Each layer should be able to stand alone while supporting the others. If any layer feels weak or disconnected, keep refining until the pyramid is rock solid.

### [1. Top of the pyramid: Start with the answer](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ux-case-study-minto-pyramid-structure-guide\#3179a8800c354ebeb9a67d84d6dffdff "1. Top of the pyramid: Start with the answer") **1\. Top of the pyramid: Start with the answer**

#### [What is it?](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ux-case-study-minto-pyramid-structure-guide\#903248410351437bb5c0e7903e6ec3a1 "What is it?") What is it?

The "answer" is your knockout punch—the single most impressive result from your project.

It's not your process, your role, or even the problem. It's the tangible impact you delivered.

#### [Core components](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ux-case-study-minto-pyramid-structure-guide\#14d1152af807405984ec16c5396e6631 "Core components") Core components

1. Time frame

2. Business impact

3. Quantifiable metric

4. Clear connection to design work

#### [Why this matters](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ux-case-study-minto-pyramid-structure-guide\#7ac8933267124e46a84f87ecd7c382ee "Why this matters") Why this matters

- Hiring managers can instantly assess project scale

- Shows you can connect design to outcomes

- Proves you understand business metrics

- Makes your case study memorable

#### [Examples](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ux-case-study-minto-pyramid-structure-guide\#2e275728fd304be48b1cdf661ef361a7 "Examples") Examples

- **Enterprise UX**

❌ Redesigned the help desk system for better efficiency.

✅ Reduced ticket resolution time by 47%, saving 1,200 employee hours monthly.

- **Mobile App**

❌ Created a new onboarding flow with improved UX.

✅ Boosted user activation by 34% through a streamlined 3-step onboarding, adding 50,000 monthly active users.

- **E-commerce**

❌ Implemented a responsive checkout design.

✅ Increased mobile conversion by 28%, generating $890,000 additional revenue in Q1.

### [2. Middle of the pyramid: Support with key arguments](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ux-case-study-minto-pyramid-structure-guide\#25e229ae7c554aa6ab1725d948b57ae9 "2. Middle of the pyramid: Support with key arguments") **2\. Middle of the pyramid: Support with key arguments**

#### [What is it?](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ux-case-study-minto-pyramid-structure-guide\#349406b85fa847bf8dc471911f61e25b "What is it?") What is it?

Your key arguments are the 2-3 main decisions or insights that led to your success.

Think of them as the bridge between your big result and how you got there.

- **Design System**

❌ Created components based on user needs and best practices.

✅ Design system impact driven by three key decisions:

1. Reduced colour variants from 47 to 12, cutting design debt by 60%

2. Standardized components, reducing developer questions by 71%

3. Implemented automated accessibility checks, eliminating 94% of WCAG violations

- **Service Design**

❌ Improved the customer service workflow through research.

✅ Three insights reshaped our service design:

1. 82% of support tickets stemmed from unclear error messages

2. Staff spent 40% of time on easily tasks that could be automated

3. Customer satisfaction dropped 23% during peak hours

#### [Layer 1 — The answer](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ux-case-study-minto-pyramid-structure-guide\#8f19e3340c2b469bba4bdfd8edd310e3 "Layer 1 — The answer") Layer 1 — The answer

Increased mobile banking adoption by 56%, driving $2.4M in cost savings through reduced branch visits.

   - Simplified transfer process → more completions.

   - Biometric login → faster, safer access.

   - Proactive error messages → fewer support tickets.
